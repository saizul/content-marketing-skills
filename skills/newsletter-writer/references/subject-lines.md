# Subject lines and preview text

## Subject line formulas

| Formula | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Specific outcome | The 20-minute brief that fixed our calendar |
| Number | 3 questions every content brief answers |
| Curiosity with a real payoff | Why we stopped posting on Mondays |
| Direct and plain | Your Q1 content plan (template inside) |
| Personal | I got this wrong for two years |
| Question | How many posts did you publish last month? |
| Series label | Content Notes #42: repurposing one post 4 ways |

## Rules

- 25-50 characters; the first 30-40 carry the message on mobile.
- Sentence case. No ALL CAPS words, at most one `!` or `?`.
- No fake `RE:` / `FWD:` and nothing that misrepresents the content (deceptive subject
  lines are prohibited by the US CAN-SPAM Act).
- Avoid spammy phrases ("free gift", "act now", "guaranteed", "100% free"); the script
  lists the ones it checks.
- Emoji: optional; one at most, never replacing a word. Many B2B lists do better without.
- Consistency beats cleverness: subscribers open because of the sender name first.

## Preview text (preheader)

- 40-130 characters; clients show roughly 35-140 depending on device and app.
- Continues the subject: adds the second half of the promise, a detail, or the payoff.
- Never repeats the subject, never "View this email in your browser".
- If you do not set it, the client shows the first text in the email instead.

| Subject | Preview |
| --- | --- |
| The 20-minute brief that fixed our calendar | One template, three questions and the links to add before anyone writes. |
| Why we stopped posting on Mondays | The data from 14 client accounts, and what we do instead. |
